01 / Executive summary

A strong experiment, with a clear path to a reliable playground

What this repo does: a compact falling-sand sandbox that combines cave generation with water, sand, wood, fire and smoke. A single Rust world and egui interface target both desktop and the browser.

The small codebase is understandable and worth evolving. Native builds succeed. The main obstacles are material-state corruption, debug fire crashes and a broken fresh web build. Resolve those before layering on more reactions. There is no evidence that an engine rewrite is needed.

Simulation data flow

A permitted tick runs sand → water → fire → smoke → wood. Each phase clones current data into tmp, reads mostly data, writes tmp, then swaps arrays. This is sequential phase buffering, not one synchronous double-buffered tick: later phases see earlier changes. Fire-generated smoke therefore ages/moves in the same tick; water-displaced wood participates in the final wood phase. Preserve or explicitly redefine that behavior when reorganizing updates.

Water stores a scalar quantity, flows down, sideways and upward under compression. MAX_MASS=10 is a preferred full-cell scale, not a hard cap; painting creates 1 unit and subsequent drags add 2. Sand falls or tries diagonals, swaps vertically with water, and displaces water to a neighboring cell for diagonal motion. Wood falls into air and is moved by water pressure; it is a per-cell material, not a connected rigid body. Fire falls into air; on support it produces smoke and ignites orthogonal wood. Smoke rises into air and loses 0.01 scalar lifetime per tick. These are stylized rules, not a thermodynamic model.

Cave generation

Generation independently assigns Dirt with probability threshold and otherwise air, then enforces a wall. Smooth counts eight neighbors, treats out-of-grid positions as solid, sets Dirt for more than four solid neighbors, air for fewer than four, and retains the current terrain at four. Non-terrain material types are copied through. One pass costs O(8N²), hence O(N²), with no connectivity analysis or guaranteed playable chamber.

Threshold endpoints are well-defined (0: empty interior; 1: filled). Repeated smoothing is manual. A proper solid border remained solid in the targeted test; all edge cells already have at least five outside/border neighbors. Several simulation and renderer reads rely on this invariant. World::new(0) and arbitrary edge material mutation are not supported by safe guards, but the current App always creates a 100² world and protects the border from painting. These are extension constraints, not demonstrated crashes in ordinary UI use.

Keep these design choices

The shared native/browser world, contiguous ndarray storage, explicit local rules, material enum and separation between App and World are good foundations for an educational sandbox. Rust bounds checks turn many invalid accesses into diagnosable panics. The existing inverse view transform is sound for tested pan/scale inputs. Keep these strengths while narrowing World’s responsibilities.

04 / Correctness & reliability

Protect material quantities before adding reactions

Findings below are reproduced against unchanged rule implementations in a temporary copy. The review module only constructs small worlds and calls existing phases. F08 contains a measured aesthetic/symmetry concern; it is not a claim that the project promised physically exact fluids.

HighF03Correctness / indexing

Supported fire panics in debug builds

Confidence: Confirmed · Effort: Small

src/app/world/mod.rs:567–598

Evidence
Place FireNormal at (y=5,x=3) in a bordered 7×7 world, directly above the floor. update_fire panics at line 586: attempt to add with overflow. A negative signed neighbor offset is cast to usize before addition. The same interior probe passes in the measured release profile because arithmetic wraps.
Practical impact
The normal cargo run development experience crashes once fire reaches support. Debug and release disagree; release wrapping is an unsafe way to express neighboring coordinates.
Recommended improvement
Add signed offsets before converting to indices, with explicit bounds handling or interior-only iteration. Keep a floor-fire regression in debug and release and exercise all four neighbors.
HighF04Correctness / conservation

Water cleanup erases incoming water

Confidence: Confirmed · Effort: Small

src/app/world/mod.rs:400–425

Evidence
A 1.0-unit Water cell directly above a 0.005-unit Water cell loses all 1.005 units in one update_water pass. The upper cell credits tmp; later, the lower source is below MIN_MASS in data, so cleanup resets its already-credited tmp cell to None and zero. Reproduced in debug and release.
Practical impact
Water disappears by far more than the intended tiny-mass cutoff. Conservation and pressure experiments become unreliable.
Recommended improvement
Apply cleanup to the completed destination state after all transfers, or preserve incoming deltas when normalizing small old sources. Test total mass including any explicitly accounted sink.
HighF05Correctness / buffering

Smooth swaps live cells with stale scalar state

Confidence: Confirmed · Effort: Small

src/app/world/mod.rs:213–254

Evidence
On a new bordered 7×7 world, set a Water cell to mass 7 and press Smooth (equivalent probe calls smooth). Its type is copied into tmp, but mass is not; swapping whole arrays turns its mass into 0. Existing buffers may instead restore mass from an earlier phase. Smoke lifetime shares the same field.
Practical impact
A cave-editing operation silently changes live material quantities and can revive older scalar values.
Recommended improvement
Start smoothing from a full current-state copy or write complete cells, then change only terrain types. Define whether smoothing is terrain-only and preserve all unrelated fields.
MediumF06Correctness / state reset

Generation leaves hidden water and smoke mass

Confidence: Confirmed · Effort: Small

src/app/world/mod.rs:201–210

Evidence
Set Water mass to 7, choose threshold 0 and generate: the interior cell becomes None but still has mass 7. rand_generate only assigns cell_type. Water flow subsequently reads neighboring mass even on None cells.

HighF07Correctness / material displacement

Buoyancy overwrites water above wood

Confidence: Confirmed · Effort: Medium

src/app/world/mod.rs:325–343, src/app/world/mod.rs:513–544

Evidence
A supported 20-unit Water cell below Wood, with another 5-unit Water cell above the wood and horizontal outlets blocked, starts with total water 25. MediumF08Correctness / directional bias

Water and wood rules prefer one direction

Confidence: Confirmed · Effort: Small

src/app/world/mod.rs:432–505

Evidence
The right-side guard accepts Wood, but the left guard only accepts Water/None, making its later Wood handler unreachable for normal state. In a symmetric 7×7 basin, an isolated 10-unit water source sends 2.5 right and 1.875 left on its first pass because the right transfer consumes remaining_mass first. Both behaviors reproduced in both profiles.
Practical impact
Mirrored scenes behave differently and floating obstacles move asymmetrically. This is a rule-quality issue, separate from conservation failures.
Recommended improvement
Share left/right eligibility and transfer logic; alternate or seed the lateral evaluation order. Define acceptable symmetry statistically rather than forcing each stochastic tick to mirror exactly.

Other rule observations and limits

Small-mass deletion at MIN_MASS=0.01 is itself an intentional numerical sink; distinguish it from F04’s loss of incoming full-cell water. MIN_DRAW=0.1 hides small visible quantities without removing them, so “no blue pixel” does not mean “no water.” A just-drained source may remain typed Water until the next phase, temporarily blocking particles. These are normalization questions to define and test, rather than additional proven high-severity findings.

Sand’s fixed top-to-bottom scan and wood’s old-state gravity rules can influence settling rates, but the two stacked-sand probes conserved grains. Random diagonal choices only happen when both paths qualify. ThreadRng is not seedable through the public world API, and generation creates another thread RNG; deterministic replay needs an owned seeded RNG. No threading exists in this code, so there is no demonstrated data race. Parallel updates would introduce conflict-resolution requirements.

Unchecked y±1/x±1 accesses in rendering and phases depend on a Dirt border. x+2 water/wood accesses are safe while a neighbor inside that border can be Wood; they are not general rectangular-world guards. Keep boundary invariants explicit and test them after each editor operation before supporting arbitrary grid dimensions, imported worlds or boundary tools.

05 / Performance & scalability

Cut geometry and copying before reaching for compute shaders

The current UI fixes the world at 100² cells; the block-size slider changes visual scale, not simulation resolution. All five material phases scan the whole world, and rendering scans it again. Cost is roughly O(N²) per tick/repaint for an N×N world; wood_stuck can additionally scan up a column, giving an O(N³) pessimistic water/wood case. The latter is a static worst-case observation, not a measured bottleneck.

Optimization order and decision gates

  1. Baseline representative scenes: empty, settled cave, falling sand, circulating water and pressured wood. Record tick p50/p95, shape/tessellation time, allocation count and uploaded bytes separately.
  2. Remove unnecessary work: skip air and offscreen cells, reuse shape/scratch storage, avoid continuous paused repaints. Retain tests for visual backgrounds and phase state.
  3. Try a persistent texture: colorize the grid into an existing image, update its handle, draw one quad. Compare at 100²/256²/512² on native and browser. Do not recreate texture IDs every frame. egui 0.17 already exposes load_texture and TextureHandle::set/set_partial; exact filtering controls vary across versions.
  4. Active tiles only if needed: sleeping settled regions must wake neighbors for mass, pressure, heat and brush edits. A tile scheduler is more complex than replacing clone allocation and cannot assume a visually still fluid is inactive.
  5. GPU rendering before GPU simulation: a fragment pass for color/glow can help presentation without changing rules. Compute simulation requires deterministic conflict resolution, mass atomics/reductions or staged kernels, readback strategy and a browser compatibility/fallback plan. It is an ambitious option, not the default fix.

README says web is slower than native, but the browser binary could not be built in the reviewed graph. There is no measured native-versus-WASM comparison in this review. Keep the CPU reference model for reproducibility even if an optional accelerated path is explored.

06 / Architecture & code quality

Separate testable rules from view state, in small steps

World currently owns data, scratch storage, RNG, clock throttling, cave generation, material rules, palette, view size/shift and painter geometry. This is workable at 705 lines, but the mix makes headless testing and new fields harder. The smallest useful seam is a deterministic step operation independent of screen and wall clock, with the application owning timing and the view owning transforms/rendering. No entity system, generic engine layer or plugin framework is needed.

What the current representation can support

Stone, ash, a few presets, better brushes and anchored plant cells fit the current enum-plus-grid approach. A clean step() API makes stepping and deterministic experiments easy to express. Save files should serialize logical world state rather than egui view objects or ThreadRng itself; store a versioned RNG choice/state or an explicit seed plus input history when replay requires it.

Heat and multiple fluids require more than another match arm. The current mass field cannot also represent fuel, moisture and smoke age without ambiguous resets. Add only the needed properties, for example a material-properties table plus separate temperature/fuel/age fields or sidecar grids. Keep variable fluid quantity distinct from particle count and gas lifetime. Choose a simple density order and a tested displacement policy before introducing oil, lava or steam.

Define the units first: one cell of water currently has a preferred mass scale of 10, while brush placement starts at 1. Turning water into steam should preserve a defined quantity even if gas occupies several cells; disappearing smoke is not a valid water model. Use a documented approximation for heat exchange, phase-change energy and gas expansion rather than claiming physical realism. Bound diffusion/substeps and reaction work so expensive scenes cannot stall the UI.

Three coherent experiments worth targeting

First

Dam & raft

Seeded cave reservoir, adjustable brush and step mode. Sand sinks while wood floats; a user-built dam breaks predictably. This turns bug fixes into a visible, repeatable milestone.

Next

Boiler cavern

Fire heats water below a cold ceiling; steam rises and condenses into a collection channel. A temperature overlay reveals the loop. Add ice only once heat and quantities behave.

Later

Living watershed

Rain feeds plants, roots slow erosion, fire leaves ash and sediment builds new banks. Let a handful of local rules create a changing ecosystem; add challenges as optional presets.

Sandbox tools and feedback

Group materials by terrain, particles, liquids, gases and energy. Expose brush size/shape, density or placement amount, an eyedropper, pause/step, tick rate and scene seed. Use a cell inspector for type, fluid quantity, temperature and recent movement. A debug overlay for active cells, mass deltas and total-water drift will make emergent behavior understandable. Replay needs deterministic randomness plus an input event log; a full history buffer can be bounded or checkpointed.

Do not attempt connected rigid-body logs, realistic Navier–Stokes fluids, a full chemistry engine and GPU compute in the same milestone. Those change the model substantially and are not prerequisites for impressive local interactions.

Identity: clean, calm experiments. Cool off-white/slate background, cyan water, ochre sand and clearly labeled heat/gas overlays. Material quantity remains legible.

Usability and accessibility details

Use visible material names, strong selected states and patterned/symbol cues in addition to color.
